# Pre-specified performance endpoints (`scripts/repro.py`)

Written and committed **before** any data of the reproducibility study were collected. The analysis in
`scripts/repro.py analyze` computes exactly these quantities. Anything else it prints (other sizes, other
pairs) is exploratory and is labelled as such.

## Implementations

- **SweepFFT** = `pmfft`: the measuring planner, as shipped.
- **Comparators:**
  - `mkl`: MKL DFTI, sequential, out-of-place;
  - `fftw_measure`: FFTW 3.3.10, MEASURE, machine wisdom imported, planning capped at 60 s;
  - `ducc`: ducc0.
- **Secondary variant:** `pmfft_heur`, SweepFFT without the measuring planner.

## Sizes

- **P (primary):** the memory-bound powers of two n = 2^22, 2^23, 2^24, 2^25, 2^26, which have a working set
  32n = 128 MB to 2 GB. On a machine whose RAM cannot hold 2^26, the largest size is
  `scripts/run_machine.sh`'s KMAX.
- **Q (secondary):** the out-of-cache smooth sizes that are not powers of two, n = 3·2^20, 5·2^20, 3·2^22,
  5·2^22.

## Measurement

- **Blocks:** 10 blocks. A block measures every (implementation, size) once, each in a fresh process pinned
  to one core.
- **Order:** within a block the order of sizes is shuffled (fixed seed), and the order of implementations
  for each size follows a row of a Williams Latin square.
- **Sessions:** blocks may be spread over several sessions (separate invocations, reboots or days); the
  session of every block is recorded.
- **Within a run:**
  - planning, then one untimed warm-up call;
  - samples of at least 2 ms each (back-to-back calls for small n);
  - at least 5 samples and at least 1.0 s of timed calls.
- **Run time t:** the median sample. Every sample is preserved.
- **Recorded with every run:**
  - effective core clock (cycles / timed seconds, from perf, when permitted);
  - package and core temperature before and after the run;
  - AC power state;
  - wall-clock time.

## Primary endpoints

- **E1:** geometric-mean speedup of SweepFFT over MKL across the sizes P.
- **E2:** geometric-mean speedup of SweepFFT over FFTW MEASURE across the sizes P. A size at which FFTW's
  planner hits its time limit in any block is excluded from E2, and the exclusion is reported.

### How the endpoints are computed

- **Per block:** for each block b and size n, the speedup is r_b(n) = t_lib / t_SweepFFT, with both runs from
  block b. The block's endpoint value is the geometric mean of r_b(n) over the sizes.
- **Estimate:** the geometric mean of the per-block endpoint values over the blocks.
- **Uncertainty:** a 95% percentile bootstrap interval over blocks, using 10,000 resamples of whole blocks
  (all sizes of a block stay together) with a fixed seed.
- **Test:** a one-sided exact sign-flip permutation test of "speedup > 1" on the per-block log endpoint
  values. Holm correction is applied across E1 and E2.

### Decision rule

SweepFFT is reported as faster on an endpoint only if both hold:

- the lower bound of the 95% bootstrap interval is > 1;
- the Holm-adjusted p-value is < 0.05.

Otherwise the result is reported as "no detectable difference" or "slower", together with the interval.

### On AMD processors

MKL may not use its fastest code paths on AMD processors. On those machines, E2 is the primary endpoint and
E1 is reported as secondary.

## Secondary endpoints

Each is the same statistic: the estimate with a 95% bootstrap interval, and no multiplicity correction.

- **S1:** SweepFFT vs ducc0 on P.
- **S2:** `pmfft_heur` (no measuring planner) vs MKL, and vs FFTW, on P.
- **S3:** SweepFFT vs MKL, and vs FFTW, on Q. FFTW sizes whose plan was capped are excluded, as for E2.
- **S4:** per-size speedups for every size in P and Q, with 95% bootstrap intervals.

## Data handling

- **No outlier removal.** A run is excluded only if its process fails or its result is invalid. A failed run
  is retried once in the same block, and the retry is logged.
- **Raw data are preserved:**
  - every run in `results/repro.csv`;
  - every sample in `results/repro_samples.csv`.
- **Reported alongside the endpoints:**
  - **Noise:** the within-run spread of samples, and the between-block coefficient of variation of t per
    (implementation, size).
  - **Drift:** the slope of log t against block index, with a 95% CI, per (implementation, size); the same
    slope for the paired log speedup; and the range of effective clock speed and temperature.

## Amendment 1 (2026-10-10, before any study data were analysed)

**What changed:** the FFTW comparator now imports `results/fftw_wisdom_fresh.dat`, not the machine's
long-lived wisdom file. That file was generated by a fresh FFTW MEASURE search for every size in P and Q,
plus 2^20, with planning capped at 600 s per size.

**Why:**
- The protocol-sensitivity study (`results/fairness.md`) showed the long-lived wisdom was stale at 2^24:
  - imported wisdom gave 8.9 GFlop/s;
  - a fresh MEASURE search gave 11.2 GFlop/s.
- With the stale wisdom, E2 would have overstated SweepFFT's advantage.

**The aborted start:** 14 runs were collected with the old wisdom, then the study was stopped. They are kept
unanalysed in `results/aborted/` and are not part of the study.

**Unchanged:** the endpoints, sizes, design, analysis and decision rule. Sizes where the 600 s cap was hit are
excluded from the FFTW endpoints, as before.

## Amendment 2 (2026-10-10, before any study data were collected)

Amendment 1 replaced FFTW's long-lived wisdom with a fresh MEASURE search. That search was not uniformly
better: at 2^23 it found an 8.6 GFlop/s plan against the old wisdom's 11.4. FFTW_MEASURE chooses by noisy
timings.

**How FFTW's plan is chosen:**
- FFTW gets its **fastest available plan per size**.
- `scripts/fftw_best_wisdom.py` timed FFTW with both wisdom files (old and fresh) in 3 alternating rounds of
  fresh processes, and recorded the faster file per size in `results/fftw_wisdom_choice.csv`.
- The choice uses only FFTW's own speed.
- The study scripts import that file for each size (`$FFTW_WISDOM_CHOICE`).

**Which FFTW comparisons count:**
- A size counts as FFTW-capped if its fresh 600 s search hit the limit (`search_capped`): 5·2^20, 3·2^22 and
  5·2^22.
- Those sizes are excluded from the FFTW endpoints (S3), exactly as a cap hit during a block would have been.
  Plans imported from wisdom take about 0 s to plan, so a cap can no longer show up during a block.
- All sizes in P completed their search within 104 s.
- 3·2^20 completed in 515 s and is included. Its complete plan runs at 16.8 GFlop/s; the plans used in all
  earlier experiments were truncated and ran at 4.8 GFlop/s.

**Unchanged:** endpoints, sizes, design, analysis and decision rule.

## Amendment 3 (2026-10-11, before any data of the second study were collected)

The first study (`results/repro.csv`, run under amendment 2) put the caller's input and output arrays on 2 MB
pages (`MADV_HUGEPAGE`). A later A/B (`scripts/caller_pages.py`, `results/caller_pages.md`) showed that this
choice changes the libraries' speed: with huge pages, the power-of-two row stride of a strided pass maps every row
to the same L2 sets (`results/REPORT.md` §20), and at 2^22 MKL and FFTW are 16–45% faster with ordinary arrays.
An ordinary caller that mallocs its arrays gets 4 KB pages under the stock THP mode (`madvise`).

**Second study, the primary one from now on:** identical protocol (endpoints, sizes, implementations, design,
10 blocks, analysis, decision rule) with the caller's arrays allocated the ordinary way
(`BENCH_CALLER_PAGES=plain`: 64-byte aligned, no paging advice). Library-internal memory is untouched.
Outputs: `results/repro_plain.csv`, `results/repro_plain_samples.csv`, `results/repro_plain.md`.

The first study is kept unchanged and reported as the huge-page protocol. Both are reported; neither is dropped.
